Three borrowers. Three situations that sound impossible.
Spoiler: they're not.

Scenario 1 of 3

The Job-Hopping Veteran

Six jobs in two years. Zero confidence.

The Client
A disabled veteran with a Certificate of Eligibility who can buy with zero down. He's had 6 jobs in the last 2 years and is 100% disabled. His spouse thinks homeownership is a pipe dream.
The Problem
"I have no stability. Six jobs in two years? I'm unqualified. And I'm disabled — who would give me a loan?"
The Solution

The Job-Hopping Veteran

What Tiffani Found
She walked through his 2-year work history. Six jobs — but every single one was in the same industry. And there was never more than a 3-day gap between positions.

That showed consistency, not chaos. He qualified for a VA loan with his Certificate of Eligibility.
The Bonus Win
Because he's 100% disabled, Tiffani also got his property taxes removed entirely. More money in his pocket every month.
Scenario 2 of 3

The Contingent Buyer

Stuck between two homes in a hot market.

The Client
Owns a home in Herriman and wants to move to Cottonwood Heights — a more coveted, competitive area. Her agent told her contingent offers won't even be looked at.
The Problem
"I have to sell before I can buy. But I can't buy without selling first. It's a chicken-and-egg problem in the most competitive market around."
The Solution

The Contingent Buyer

What Tiffani Found
Her debt-to-income on a conventional loan was too tight — she'd need to sell first. But on an FHA loan, the DTI limits are more flexible.

She could qualify carrying both mortgage payments at once. That meant buying Cottonwood Heights without a sale contingency.
The Bonus Win
She closed on the new house, had 2 full weeks to move, then sold the Herriman home. No bridge loan. No double move.
Scenario 3 of 3

The Cash-Strapped Investor

Great deal, no cash, self-employed.

The Client
Found a $400,000 investment property with huge potential. Needs 25% down — about $100,000. Self-employed, so traditional income verification doesn't work.
The Problem
"I own a property free and clear, but I need that equity to buy this new one. I'm self-employed — no traditional lender counts my income. Without $100K liquid, this deal dies."
The Solution

The Cash-Strapped Investor

What Tiffani Found
He owned an investment property free and clear. Tiffani structured a cash-out DSCR loan on that property.

DSCR = Debt Service Coverage Ratio. Based on the property's future rental income, not his personal income. Perfect for self-employed investors.
The Bonus Win
He pulled $100K from the paid-off property as his down payment. The new property had two entrances — two families at ~$2,500 total, covering the new mortgage.

Zero personal income used. Zero cash out of pocket.
The Pattern

What All Three Have in Common

The "No" They Heard Before
Every client had been told "impossible" before they found Tiffani. The veteran was ready to give up. The buyer was told she'd never get into Cottonwood Heights. The investor thought his deal was dead.

They weren't impossible. They just needed the right loan program.
The Lesson
VA loans. FHA flexibility. DSCR for investors. Most lenders show you one path. Tiffani finds the path that actually works for your situation.
