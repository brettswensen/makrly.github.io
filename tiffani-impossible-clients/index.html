Scenario 1 of 3

The Job-Hopping Veteran

Read This Aloud

"I'm Jake. I'm a veteran, and I have my Certificate of Eligibility, so I know I can buy a home with zero money down. But I've got a problem — over the past two years, I've had six different jobs.

On paper, I look like a mess. And I'm 100% disabled, which I worry makes me even riskier to lenders. My spouse has already told me homeownership is a pipe dream. I don't think I'll ever qualify."

The Resolution

Jake's work history told a different story than the numbers suggested. Every job was in the same industry, and he never had more than a 3-day gap between positions. That's consistency — not chaos.

He qualified for a VA loan using his Certificate of Eligibility. Six jobs and all.

Bonus win: Because he's 100% disabled, his property taxes were removed entirely. Lower monthly payment, more money in his pocket every month.

Scenario 2 of 3

The Contingent Buyer

Read This Aloud

"I'm Emily. I own a home in Herriman, but I want to move to Cottonwood Heights. It's where I want to be — better schools, closer to work, just a better fit for my family.

But my agent just told me the hard truth: contingent offers won't even be looked at in that market. Too competitive. I have to sell my current home before I can buy a new one. But I can't buy without selling first. I'm stuck in a catch-22."

The Resolution

Emily's debt-to-income on a conventional loan was too tight — she'd need to sell first. But FHA has more flexible DTI limits.

She could qualify carrying both mortgage payments at the same time. That meant buying in Cottonwood Heights without a sale contingency — the exact thing her agent said was impossible.

Bonus win: She closed on the new house, had two full weeks to move, then sold the Herriman home. No bridge loan. No double move. No storage unit.

Scenario 3 of 3

The Cash-Strapped Investor

Read This Aloud

"I'm Tom. I found a $400,000 investment property — fairly priced, great location, huge upside. I need 25% down, about $100,000. But I don't have that kind of cash sitting around.

I'm also self-employed. My income is solid, but it doesn't fit in a W-2 box, so traditional lenders don't know what to do with me. I own another property free and clear, but I need that equity to buy this new one. Without $100K liquid, this deal dies."

The Resolution

Tom had untapped equity in his paid-off investment property. We structured a cash-out DSCR loan on that property.

DSCR = Debt Service Coverage Ratio. It qualifies based on the property's future rental income, not personal income. Perfect for self-employed investors.

He pulled $100K from the existing property for his down payment. The new property has two separate entrances — two families at ~$2,500 total rent, covering the new mortgage.

Bonus win: Zero personal income used. Zero cash out of pocket. One smart loan structure. His deal lived.

They weren't impossible. They just needed the right loan program.

VA loans. FHA flexibility. DSCR for investors. Most lenders show you one path. I find the path that actually works for your situation.
