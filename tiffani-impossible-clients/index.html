Scenario 1 of 3

The Job-Hopping Veteran

The Situation

Marcus walked into my office a nervous wreck. He's a veteran who served our country and has his Certificate of Eligibility — meaning he can buy a home with zero money down. But he was convinced he'd never qualify.

Over the past two years, he's had six different jobs. On paper, that looks like chaos. To make matters worse, he's 100% disabled and worried lenders would see him as high-risk. His spouse had already written off homeownership as a pipe dream.

"I have no stability. Six jobs in two years? I'm unqualified. And I'm disabled — who would give me a loan?"
The Resolution

I sat down with Marcus and walked through his full work history. Here's what jumped out: every single job was in the same industry. And when he switched employers, there was never more than a 3-day gap. That's not instability — that's consistency.

He qualified for a VA loan using his Certificate of Eligibility. Six jobs and all.

And because he's 100% disabled, I got his property taxes removed entirely. His monthly payment dropped even further — more money in his pocket every single month.

Scenario 2 of 3

The Contingent Buyer

The Situation

Sarah owns a home in Herriman but her heart is set on Cottonwood Heights — a more coveted area where homes move fast and competition is fierce. Her real estate agent delivered the hard truth: contingent offers won't even be looked at in that market.

She has to sell before she can buy. But she can't buy without selling first. Classic catch-22.

"I have to sell before I can buy. But I can't buy without selling first. It's a chicken-and-egg problem in the most competitive market around."
The Resolution

I ran her numbers on a conventional loan first. Her debt-to-income was too tight — she'd need to sell her Herriman home before buying anything.

But then we looked at FHA. FHA has more flexible DTI limits, and I realized: she could qualify carrying both mortgage payments at the same time. That meant she could buy the Cottonwood Heights home without a sale contingency.

She closed on the new house, had two full weeks to pack and move, then sold the Herriman home. No bridge loan. No double move. No storage unit. A seamless transition her agent thought was impossible.

Scenario 3 of 3

The Cash-Strapped Investor

The Situation

David found a $400,000 investment property — fairly priced, huge upside potential, exactly the kind of deal investors dream about. He needs 25% down, roughly $100,000. Problem: he doesn't have $100K liquid.

He's also self-employed, which means his income doesn't fit neatly into a W-2 box. Traditional lenders see him as complicated at best.

"I own a property free and clear, but I need that equity to buy this new one. I'm self-employed — no traditional lender counts my income. Without $100K liquid, this deal dies."
The Resolution

I asked about his assets. Turns out he owns another investment property free and clear. That's untapped equity sitting there.

I structured a cash-out DSCR loan on that paid-off property. DSCR = Debt Service Coverage Ratio — it qualifies based on the property's future rental income, not his personal income. Perfect for self-employed investors.

He pulled $100K from the existing property and used it as his down payment. The new property has two separate entrances — we rent to two families for about $2,500 total, which covers the new mortgage payment.

Zero personal income used. Zero cash out of pocket. One smart loan structure. His deal lived.

They weren't impossible. They just needed the right loan program.
