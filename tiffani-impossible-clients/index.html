Scenario 1 of 3

The Job-Hopping Veteran

🙋‍♂️

Volunteer: Read this card. You're about to meet your lender and you think your situation is hopeless.

👤 Meet the Client
You're a veteran who served in the military. You have your Certificate of Eligibility and know you can buy with zero down. But there's a problem: you've had 6 jobs in the last 2 years.

You're also 100% disabled and worried no lender will touch you. Your spouse thinks buying a home is a pipe dream.
❌ The "Impossible" Problem
"I have no stability. Six jobs in two years? I'm unqualified. And I'm disabled — who would give me a loan?"
💬 Ask the Group
Is this actually impossible? What would you do?
The Reveal

The Job-Hopping Veteran

✅ The Strategy
I sat down and walked through his 2-year work history. Yes, 6 jobs. But here's what mattered: every job was in the same industry, and there was never more than a 3-day gap between positions.

That showed consistency, not chaos. He qualified for a VA loan with his Certificate of Eligibility.
🎁 The Bonus Win
Because he's 100% disabled, we also got his property taxes removed entirely. His monthly payment dropped even further.

What felt impossible became his dream home — with money left over every month.

Scenario 2 of 3

The Contingent Buyer

🙋‍♀️

Volunteer: You're living in Herriman but your heart is set on Cottonwood Heights — and your agent just told you it's never going to happen.

👤 Meet the Client
You own a home in Herriman and want to move to Cottonwood Heights. It's a more coveted area with hotter competition.

Your real estate agent says: "If your offer is contingent on selling your Herriman house, they won't even look at it."
❌ The "Impossible" Problem
"I have to sell before I can buy. But I can't buy without selling first. It's a chicken-and-egg problem in the most competitive market around."
💬 Ask the Group
Is this actually impossible? What's the workaround?
The Reveal

The Contingent Buyer

✅ The Strategy
We looked at her debt-to-income ratio on a conventional loan. It was tight — she'd need to sell first.

But then we explored FHA. With FHA's more flexible DTI limits, she could qualify carrying BOTH mortgage payments.

That meant she could buy the Cottonwood Heights home without a contingency.
🎁 The Bonus Win
She closed on the new house, had 2 weeks to move, then sold the Herriman home — no bridge loan, no double move, no storage unit.

Her agent's "impossible" became a seamless transition from one home to the next.
🦸‍♀️ Mortgage Superhero Strikes Again
Scenario 3 of 3

The Cash-Strapped Investor

🙋‍♂️

Volunteer: You're a self-employed investor with a paid-off property and a can't-miss deal. But there's a $100,000 problem.

👤 Meet the Client
You found a $400,000 investment property that's fairly priced and has great potential. You need 25% down — about $100,000.

Problem: you don't have $100K liquid. And you're self-employed, so traditional income verification is a nightmare.
❌ The "Impossible" Problem
"I have assets, but not cash. My income is solid but doesn't fit in a W-2 box. Without a big down payment, this deal dies."
💬 Ask the Group
Is this actually impossible? How do you buy real estate with no cash down and no traditional income?
The Reveal

The Cash-Strapped Investor

✅ The Strategy
He owned another investment property free and clear. We did a cash-out DSCR loan on that property.

DSCR = Debt Service Coverage Ratio. It's based on the property's future rental income, not his personal income. Perfect for self-employed investors.
🎁 The Bonus Win
He pulled $100K from the paid-off property as his down payment. The new property had two entrances — we could rent to two families for ~$2,500 total, covering the new mortgage.

Zero personal income used. Zero cash out of pocket. One smart loan structure.

The Pattern
