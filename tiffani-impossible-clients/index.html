Scenario 1 of 3

The Job-Hopping Veteran

Jake's story — why it seemed impossible

"I'm Jake. I'm a veteran, and I have my Certificate of Eligibility, so I know I can buy a home with zero money down. But I've got a problem — over the past two years, I've had six different jobs.

On paper, I look like a mess. I'm also 100% disabled, which I worry makes me even riskier to lenders. My spouse has already told me homeownership is a pipe dream."

Why It Seemed Impossible
❌ 6 jobs in 2 years = looks unstable
❌ 100% disabled = perceived as high-risk
❌ No lender would touch him
The Resolution

The Job-Hopping Veteran

How each issue was solved

Issue 1: 6 Jobs in 2 Years
✅ Every job was in the same industry
✅ Never more than a 3-day gap between jobs = consistency, not chaos
✅ Qualified for a VA loan with COE
Issue 2: 100% Disabled
✅ Property taxes removed entirely
✅ Lower monthly payment, more money in pocket

Jake bought his home. What looked like a pipe dream became reality — with money left over every month.

Scenario 2 of 3

The Contingent Buyer

Emily's story — why it seemed impossible

"I'm Emily. I own a home in Herriman, but I want to move to Cottonwood Heights. It's where I want to be — better schools, closer to work, just a better fit for my family.

But my agent just told me the hard truth: contingent offers won't even be looked at in that market. I have to sell my current home before I can buy a new one. But I can't buy without selling first. I'm stuck in a catch-22."

Why It Seemed Impossible
❌ Contingent offers rejected in hot market
❌ Conventional DTI too tight — can't carry two mortgages
❌ Must sell first, but needs somewhere to live
The Resolution

The Contingent Buyer

How each issue was solved

Issue 1: Can't Get Offer Accepted
✅ Switched from conventional to FHA
✅ FHA's flexible DTI = could carry both mortgages
✅ Made offer without a contingency
Issue 2: Sell Before Buy
✅ Closed on new house first
✅ Had 2 weeks to move
✅ Sold Herriman home after moving out
✅ No bridge loan. No double move.

Emily got her Cottonwood Heights home. Her agent's "impossible" became a seamless transition.

Scenario 3 of 3

The Cash-Strapped Investor

Tom's story — why it seemed impossible

"I'm Tom. I found a $400,000 investment property — fairly priced, great location, huge upside. I need 25% down, about $100,000. But I don't have that kind of cash sitting around.

I'm also self-employed. My income is solid, but it doesn't fit in a W-2 box, so traditional lenders don't know what to do with me. I own another property free and clear, but I need that equity to buy this new one. Without $100K liquid, this deal dies."

Why It Seemed Impossible
❌ Needs $100K down, no liquid cash
❌ Self-employed = no traditional income verification
❌ Has equity trapped in another property, but can't access it
The Resolution

The Cash-Strapped Investor

How each issue was solved

Issue 1: No Cash for Down Payment
✅ Structured cash-out DSCR loan on paid-off property
✅ Pulled $100K from existing equity
✅ Used as down payment on new $400K property
Issue 2: Self-Employed Income
✅ DSCR = uses property's future rental income, not personal income
✅ Self-employed status = irrelevant
Issue 3: New Property Cash Flow
✅ Two separate entrances = two rental units
✅ ~$2,500 total rent covers new mortgage

Tom closed the deal. Zero personal income used. Zero cash out of pocket. One smart loan structure.
