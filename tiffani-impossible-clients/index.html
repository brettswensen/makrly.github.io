Scenario 1 of 3

The Job-Hopping Veteran

Jake's story, why lenders said no

"Hi, I'm Jake. I'm a veteran who served our country, and I have my Certificate of Eligibility, which means I can buy a home with zero money down. I've been wanting to buy for a while.

But here's the thing, over the past two years, I've had six different jobs. They've all been in the same field, but I know how that looks on paper. I'm also 100% disabled, which adds another layer of concern. Every lender I've talked to has made it clear: I'm not their ideal borrower."

Why Traditional Lenders Said No
❌ Six job changes in two years, seen as unstable
❌ 100% disabled, perceived as complicated
❌ Assumed unqualified despite having a COE
The Solution

The Job-Hopping Veteran

Jake, how each barrier was removed

Barrier 1: Six Jobs in Two Years
✅ All jobs were in the same industry
✅ Never more than a 3-day gap between positions
✅ Underwriting saw consistency, not risk
✅ Qualified for a VA loan with his COE
Barrier 2: Disability Status
✅ Property taxes removed entirely, a benefit for 100% disabled veterans
✅ Lower monthly payment, more monthly savings

Jake bought his home. Jobs that looked like a liability on the surface told a completely different story once someone took the time to understand them.

Scenario 2 of 3

The Contingent Buyer

Emily's story, caught between two homes

"Hi, I'm Emily. I own a home in Herriman, but I've been wanting to move to Cottonwood Heights for a while, it's closer to work, better schools for my kids, just a better fit for where my family is right now.

The challenge is the market there. It's competitive. I was told that if my offer depends on selling my current home first, sellers won't even consider it. I understand where they're coming from, but that leaves me in a tough spot, I need to sell to buy, but I can't buy without selling first."

Why Traditional Lenders Said No
❌ Contingent offers rejected in competitive markets
❌ Conventional DTI too tight, can't qualify for two mortgages
❌ Seemed forced to sell first, rent, then buy
The Solution

The Contingent Buyer

Emily, how each barrier was removed

Barrier 1: Contingent Offers Get Rejected
✅ Evaluated options beyond conventional loans
✅ FHA has more flexible DTI guidelines
✅ Emily qualified to carry both mortgages
✅ Made an offer without a contingency
Barrier 2: Sell First, Then Buy
✅ Closed on the new home first
✅ Had two weeks to move at her own pace
✅ Sold the Herriman home after moving out
✅ No bridge loan. No double move.

Emily got her Cottonwood Heights home. The solution wasn't waiting for the market to change, it was finding a loan program that fit her actual situation.

Scenario 3 of 3

The Cash-Strapped Investor

Tom's story, great deal, wrong paperwork

"Hi, I'm Tom. I'm self-employed, and I've been investing in real estate for a while. I found a $400,000 property that's fairly priced with huge upside, it's exactly the kind of deal I look for. I need 25% down, about $100,000.

The issue is cash flow. I'm self-employed, my income is solid, but it doesn't look like a W-2, so traditional lenders aren't sure how to evaluate me. I own another investment property free and clear, so I know I have equity, but I haven't been able to access it in a way that works for this deal. Without $100K liquid, I'm stuck."

Why Traditional Lenders Said No
❌ Needs $100K down, no liquid cash available
❌ Self-employed, income doesn't fit traditional verification
❌ Has equity locked in another property but can't access it conventionally
The Solution

The Cash-Strapped Investor

Tom, how each barrier was removed

Barrier 1: No Cash for Down Payment
✅ Structured cash-out DSCR loan on paid-off property
✅ Pulled $100K from existing equity
✅ Used as down payment on new property
Barrier 2: Self-Employed Income
✅ DSCR loan uses property income, not personal income
✅ Self-employed status = completely irrelevant
Barrier 3: Will the New Property Cash Flow?
✅ Two separate entrances = two rental units
✅ ~$2,500 total rent covers the new mortgage
✅ Property pays for itself

Tom closed the deal. The equity was always there, it just needed the right loan structure to unlock it.
